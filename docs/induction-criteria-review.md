# Induction criteria review: current materials, concerns and the enhanced rubric

*Prepared 6 October 2026 for the South London College academic team. Everything
marked **verified** was read from the named source on the date shown. Everything
marked **inference** or **unverified** is our reading of the evidence and should
be checked by the college before it is relied on.*

## 1. What this document is

The academic team supplied three artefacts that describe how online learner
inductions are currently run and evaluated:

| Artefact | Version / date on the document | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Online Academic Induction Guidelines for Mentors* (PDF) | version 12.08.2026 | The standard mentors are expected to deliver to: preparation, appearance, delivery, closing, record-keeping |
| *Induction Programme 7* (PowerPoint, 32 slides) | undated | The deck used for the NCFE CACHE Level 5 Diploma in Leadership for Health and Social Care and Children and Young People's Services |
| *Evaluation of Online Learner Induction* (Microsoft Forms screenshot) | undated | The checklist the academic team completes after watching an induction: header fields, 10 checklist sections, an overall rating and free text |

The team's instruction was that the new system does not need to adhere to these
materials, that there may be concerns with them, and that the criteria should
be enhanced using UK best practice and an understanding of the learners South
London College enrols. This document records what we found, why the enhanced
criteria look the way they do, and what remains for the college to decide.

The enhanced criteria are installed in the system as the preset
**"Online learner induction (South London College)"** and become the default
for induction meetings. The original generic preset
("Learner induction meeting (UK further education)") is kept so that the two
can be compared and so that nothing already scored against it changes. Both
can be edited in the Criteria screen by an academic manager or administrator.

## 2. Who the induction is for: the South London College learner

This section is built from the college's public website, read on 6 October
2026. Website claims are the college's own; where we could not verify a figure
we say so.

**Verified from southlondoncollege.org (6 October 2026):**

- The college describes itself as "a leading online educational institution
  based in the UK that provides accredited academic and professional
  qualifications" offering "flexible, affordable, and career-oriented online
  courses to students globally" (FAQs page, https://southlondoncollege.org/faqs/).
- Delivery is fully online and asynchronous. The FAQs state there are no live
  classes at specific times; materials are pre-recorded or text based; learners
  study at their own pace with a named tutor, mentor or assessor reachable by
  phone, WhatsApp, email, forum and web chat (FAQs page; course pages).
- Two study plans: a Standard plan described as "for those balancing work/life"
  and a Fast Track plan for learners who can study intensively. Durations vary
  by course page (for example Standard 8 to 12 months and Fast Track 3 to 6
  months). Course access is for 12 months; extensions carry a fee listed on the
  Course Extension Fee product page at £55 to £245 depending on level
  (https://southlondoncollege.org/product/course-extension-fee/).
- Enrolment is open all year, from the UK or overseas; prices are in GBP with
  currency conversion at checkout; the site footer offers six languages; most
  courses offer interest-free instalment plans; seasonal discount banners were
  live on the day of reading.
- Awarding organisations named: Pearson (BTEC), NCFE CACHE, ATHE, CMI, Qualifi,
  Focus Awards and the non-regulated Quality Licence Scheme (QLS). The FAQs say
  most vocational courses are Ofqual-regulated; QLS pages state those courses
  are not regulated and are not accredited qualifications.
- Course listings by level on the day of reading (counts of web listings, not
  enrolments): Level 1 18, Level 2 41, Level 3 76, Level 4 76, Level 5 58,
  Level 6 32, Level 7 41.
- Assessment on almost every course is by written assignments and portfolios
  marked internally, with no exams. Care, childcare and early-years diplomas
  also require workplace observation, expert witness testimony from a manager
  and, for some, 300 to 400 placement hours.
- Entry conditions for the Level 5 care and early-years diplomas require the
  learner to be at least 19 and working as, or able to act as, a deputy
  manager or manager in a suitable setting. Every Ofqual-regulated course page
  we checked carries the sentence "We also welcome applicants without formal
  qualifications, as relevant work experience and different educational
  backgrounds are always considered."
- Knowledge-only routes (business, law, IT, accounting) explicitly welcome
  international learners; at Level 7 an English level of IELTS 5.5 or CEFR B2
  is only "recommended".

**Unverified (the college's own marketing figures):** "120,000+ Active
learners", "94% Learner Satisfaction" and "120+ Professional Instructors" on
the homepage, and "Over 12,000 ambitious individuals" on the About Us page.
These are inconsistent with each other and could not be checked. Course page
"STUDENTS" counters (for example 369 on the Level 5 Leadership diploma) are
site counters of unknown definition. Trustpilot could not be read from our
environment.

**The learner profile that follows (inference from the facts above):**

An adult, usually in work and often already a deputy manager, manager, room
leader or senior care worker in a regulated UK care, childcare or education
setting; paying privately, often by instalments; frequently returning to study
after a long gap or with no qualification above Level 2; sometimes outside the
UK or with English as an additional language; studying alone with no timetable
and a 12-month clock that costs money to extend.

The support needs that follow, and that the enhanced criteria weight most
heavily, are therefore:

1. **Assessment literacy.** Report structure, PEEL paragraphs, Harvard
   referencing, academic integrity and the Pass/Merit/Distinction criteria. The
   current deck spends eight slides on these topics, which signals where
   learners struggle.
2. **Workplace evidence.** Knowing early how observations, expert witness
   testimony and placement hours will be organised alongside the job.
3. **Time and money.** A realistic weekly study plan. From the published Total
   Qualification Time of 900 hours for the Level 5 Leadership diploma
   (Ofqual register, read 6 October 2026): 900 / 52 weeks is about 17 hours a
   week over 12 months; 900 / 39 weeks is about 23 hours a week over a 9-month
   plan; 900 / 26 weeks is about 35 hours a week over a 6-month plan. Using the
   minimum Guided Learning Hours of 515 instead: 515 / 52 is about 10 hours a
   week and 515 / 39 is about 13. These are arithmetic from the regulator's
   figures, not college statements, and the college should decide which figure
   it quotes.
4. **Plain language.** Acronyms such as RQF, GLH, TQT, LED, IQA, EYFS and PEEL
   need explaining; understanding needs checking.
5. **Digital readiness.** Login, portal navigation, Word (not PDF) submission,
   file naming, Teams/Zoom features.
6. **Isolation and momentum.** One named human contact, a communication rhythm
   and a first check-in date.
7. **Starting points and adjustments.** Prior learning, English and maths,
   disability and health needs raised sensitively and acted on, with the
   Pre-Course Learner Profile as the record.
8. **Mixed cohorts.** The evaluation form covers four brands (Global Edulink,
   South London College, 1Training, Study365) and three programme families, so
   a group induction may mix qualifications and levels; personalisation is a
   quality signal, not a nicety.

## 3. Strengths of the current materials

The review found a sound foundation that the enhanced criteria build on rather
than replace:

- The guidelines are learner-centred. Section 1 says learners "do not need to
  remember every detail" and the mentor should focus on confidence,
  expectations and knowing where to find support. The five principles
  (Engagement, Communication, Commitment, Accuracy, Inclusion) are a usable
  category skeleton.
- The guidelines' "Mandatory course-specific update check" requires the mentor
  to verify title, pathway, TQT/GLH/credits, units, assessment and progression
  against the specification before each session and not to present generic or
  previous-course information as current. This is exactly the accuracy
  standard the system can test spoken content against.
- Several observable behaviours in the guidelines convert directly into
  criteria: recording notice at the start (4.1); confirm learners can hear and
  see (4.1); invite introductions including "what they hope to achieve" (4.1);
  ask how to pronounce a name (2.4); pause to check understanding before
  technical sections (4.6); say how unanswered questions will be followed up
  (4.6); signpost transitions (4.7); close with summary, the 14-day
  enrolment-document reminder and contact details (section 5).
- The form's sections 10, 11 and 14 contain observable, well-phrased delivery
  items, and section 13 covers academic integrity, which the original generic
  preset lacked.
- The deck's objectives, entry requirements, 14-day documents, PEEL guidance,
  report structure and file-naming rules are concrete content learners need.
  Its TQT (900) and credit value (90) match the Ofqual register, and its first
  six units are the six shared core units in the NCFE factsheet.

## 5. Design principles for the enhanced criteria
1. **Score only what the learner heard and saw.** Every criterion can be evidenced from the transcript (with timestamps), the slides and the learner booklet. Preparation is scored through its observable trace, which is accurate course facts. Post-session administration (uploading the recording, the Master Tracker, sharing resources) belongs in a separate checklist and does not affect the quality score.
2. **One construct per criterion, written as behaviour.** Each criterion has 1, 3 and 5 descriptors that describe what the Mentor said or did and what the learner was asked, so two reviewers (or the AI reviewer and a human moderator) reach the same score from the same evidence.
3. **The overall grade is computed, not chosen.** Weighted percentage across scored criteria, with grade bands (Excellent, Good, Developing, Needs support). An "essential" criterion scoring below 3 caps the grade, so a polished session that omits the safeguarding contact or the recording notice cannot be graded Excellent.
4. **Weighted for this learner profile.** Course-fact accuracy and safeguarding carry weight 2. Support needs, study planning, academic integrity, active listening and personalisation carry 1.5. Housekeeping items (agenda, structure, professional conduct, feedback invitation) carry 0.5.
5. **Keep the college's vocabulary.** Mentor, Learner Portal, Pre-Course Learner Profile, assessment plan, 14-day enrolment documents, Standard and Fast Track plans, PEEL and Harvard referencing are used as the college uses them.
6. **Commercial messaging is out of scope.** No criterion rewards promotions, referrals or review requests. One low-weight criterion checks that feedback is invited neutrally from every learner.
7. **Every criterion cites its basis.** Each has up to six framework references with a note and, where the document is public, a link, so an academic manager can see why it is there and decide whether to keep it.
8. **Everything is editable.** The preset is installed once; academic managers adjust wording, weights and essential flags in the Criteria screen. Changes are versioned so earlier reports keep the criteria they were scored against.

## 6. The enhanced criteria set

Preset name in the system: **South London College online academic induction (Mentor-led, group or one-to-one)**.

Criteria for reviewing a recorded online academic induction delivered by a Mentor over Teams, Zoom or Google Meet to new learners on South London College's self-paced vocational diplomas (also usable for the GEL, 1 Training and Study 365 brands). Scored only from what the learner heard and saw in the recording: the opening and recording notice; the learner's starting points, fit and goals; accurate programme, assessment and progression information; online study readiness and support; safeguarding, wellbeing and learner rights; engagement and communication; and the close. Pre- and post-session administration and commercial messaging are deliberately excluded from the quality score and listed as out of scope.

Totals: 7 groups, 30 criteria, 13 essential, total weight 32.5.

## 8. Decisions for the college

1. **Recording and AI review notice.** Confirm the lawful basis for recording
   inductions and for AI-assisted review, add it to the privacy notice and the
   Learner Agreement, and give mentors a scripted notice (purpose, who sees it,
   retention, how to object). Until then, the system's essential criterion
   "Recording notice given correctly" will score low on every recording, which
   is the correct outcome.
2. **Commercial messaging.** Decide whether referral, promotion and review
   requests belong in an induction at all. If kept, they should be brief,
   pressure-free and never conditional on satisfaction. Have whoever owns
   consumer-law compliance check the wording against CMA208.
3. **Pre-induction information.** Move the Initial Assessment and Pre-Course
   Learner Profile before the induction wherever possible so the mentor can
   personalise the session.
4. **Programme fact sheets.** Create a one-page fact sheet per programme
   (qualification number, pathway, TQT/GLH/credits, units, assessment, plan
   terms, progression) from the awarding organisation's specification. The
   system can check spoken and slide content against it when it is uploaded as
   the "presentation" file for the meeting.
5. **Fix the Microsoft Form now** (delete duplicate sections 12 and 17) if it
   remains in use during the transition.
6. **Correct the deck** (GLH on slide 6, pathway and unit structure on slides
   5 and 9 to 10, progression on slide 29, slide 20 text, heading-only slides).
7. **Weights and essentials.** The enhanced rubric's weights and essential
   flags are our proposal from the evidence above. Academic managers should
   review them in the Criteria screen after the first ten or so reviews and
   adjust. Every change is versioned, so earlier reports stay as they were.
